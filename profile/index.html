Building the bridge between digital analytics, business intelligence and AI.

I've spent much of my career trying to answer a deceptively simple question: what is the data actually telling us, and what should we do about it?

In my current role as a Senior Digital Analyst, that question has taken me well beyond traditional web analytics. I work across Power BI, Contentsquare, Adobe Analytics, GA4 and Microsoft Copilot to understand how customers move through digital experiences, where those experiences break down, how changes affect behavior, and how to turn all of that into something people can actually use.

Over time, I've become less interested in building dashboards just to report numbers. I want to build systems that help people understand what's happening.

How I work

01

From reporting to diagnosis

A typical question starts with something like "Why did checkout performance decline yesterday?" Answering it well means finding where customers stopped progressing, whether the change was concentrated on one device or brand, whether errors or a site release played a role, and, most importantly, whether the change is actually unusual or just normal daily variation. So I treat analytics as an operating process: Detect → Localize → Diagnose → Prioritize → Validate.

02

Trustworthy numbers first

A beautiful dashboard doesn't mean very much if the logic behind it isn't right. Some of my most important work has happened underneath the visuals: what counts as an error session, which funnel step an error belongs to, what denominator a rate should use, and whether a dashboard reconciles with its source. The goal isn't technical complexity for its own sake. The goal is trust.

03

What comes after the dashboard

Analysts still spend a lot of time gathering context, comparing charts and connecting related signals by hand. I'm exploring how AI can sit on top of governed data, semantic models and business definitions to speed that up. I don't see AI replacing analysts. I see it giving analysts more time to understand information instead of assembling it.
