Case study · NBA analytics

What actually decided the game?

A box score says who won. I wanted dashboards that say why, clearly enough that a coach, an executive or a fan gets it in seconds.

  • Power BI (PBIP, TMDL)
  • DAX
  • Python
  • nba_api
  • Git / GitHub
  • HTML & SVG charts

How it's built

01

Data foundation

A daily pull from the NBA Stats API covers team and player box scores, every shot with its location, player tracking and the schedule. Raw pulls are kept as received; cleaned tables feed the model.

02

Semantic model

A Power BI model with defined metrics: possession-based offensive, defensive and net ratings, the Four Factors, shot zones and role-change signals. Rates come from season totals, never from averaging per-game values, so a 40-shot night and a 4-shot night count for what they are.

03

Rules before insights

Every metric has one written definition, and every claim has a minimum sample size. A change smaller than normal game-to-game wobble is labeled noise, not signal.

04

Dashboards and stories

Four dashboards (Game Overview, Team Ratings, Team Profile, Player Profile) refresh each morning, and data stories answer one question each, from early-season mirages to who's really changed roles.

Design decisions

One color means one thing. Orange marks the real signal, light orange a change at the edge of meaningful, and gray the noise. Home and away are teal and sky. No team colors or logos, so the data carries the meaning.

The answer goes in the title. "Second chances created the separation," then the evidence underneath.

Jargon is explained where it appears. Terms like usage, true shooting and net rating open a plain-language definition on hover.
